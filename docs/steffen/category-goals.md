# Category goals

Every case states its `category_goal`; the rates of its cost C follow it.

| Goal | Used for (`company.md`) | What it means | What becomes expensive in C | How to negotiate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `leverage` | Machined parts, sheet metal, raw material | Competitive market; we have alternatives | Mainly price. Low fallback risk (the brief's example uses 3 %) | Push price hard: rival quotes, should-cost, index evidence, bundling. Squeezing the margin costs nothing extra. |
| `stabilise` | Castings, electrical (strategic) | Long-term partner, energy and copper exposure | Relationship charge if the supplier ends up below a sustainable margin; probably a higher fallback risk | Aim for a fair price near full cost plus margin, not the minimum. Win on structure: multi-year term, a fair index clause, effective date, payment terms. |
| `secure_supply` | Seals (bottleneck); every capacity squeeze | Few sources, supply at risk | Uncovered need (high fallback risk), lead time beyond our need, plus the relationship charge | Cover the full need for the full horizon, and secure lead time and volume first. Offer security (long contract, binding volume, faster payment) for capacity. Squeezing the price risks the supply and the score. |
| `quality_first` | Quality escalations (e.g. sheet metal after the 2026 base-frame issues) | A supplier with poor quality has to fix it | Defects: committed PPM × cost per defect, minus the chargeback | Fight for a low `ppm_commitment` and a chargeback ≥ `cost_per_defect`, which zeroes the quality cost. Use scorecard and QN evidence. Price is secondary. |
| `routine` | C-parts, kits and bundles | Low value, many small items | Probably mostly price and process; small stakes | Keep it simple: consolidate into kits, standard terms, close quickly. Don't spend rounds over cents. |
